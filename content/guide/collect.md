Brief collectors on these rules before the first site. The sections below give the reasons, from SOP §2.1.

## The rules

1. **One object, one form.** Finalise the form and start a new one for the next object.
2. **Observe, don't ask.** If you cannot check it, answer "Can't see".
3. **Stand at the object for the GPS point.** Wait for the accuracy. Place the pin on the map only if the GPS will not settle.
4. **Photograph the object, not people.**
5. **Use the feature ID agreed for the site**, for example WP-01, WP-02.
6. **Put anything else in the note.**
7. **Send whenever there is a connection, and every evening.**
8. **Your location is logged while a form is open** (the audit log). Tell collectors.

## Observe, don't ask

RISA records infrastructure and services by direct observation, not interviews. Collectors note what they can see and check. If a status can only be known by asking someone, record "Can't see" and put what you were told in the note.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Working | In use and doing its job. |
| Partly working | Works with limits: damaged, restricted, some units out of order. |
| Not working | Closed, broken, destroyed or blocked. |
| Can't see | The collector could not check it. |

For an access constraint or a hazard, Working means Passable, Partly working Restricted and Not working Blocked. If the status is Partly working or Not working, the form asks what is wrong. "Can't see" is an honest answer and better than a guess.

## GPS at the object

Stand at the water point, not across the road from it. Wait for the accuracy to settle; the form warns above 10 m. If the GPS cannot get a good fix (indoors, under trees), step outside and wait. If it still won't settle, place the point by hand on the map: the form records that it was placed manually, and the daily review checks it against the imagery.

The audit log records the device's location while a form is open. It is not a continuous track of the collector's route, and it works only in the Android app, not in web forms.

Kobo's articles on [GPS questions](https://support.kobotoolbox.org/gps_questions.html) and [collecting GPS data](https://support.kobotoolbox.org/collect_gps.html), and on [audit metadata](https://support.kobotoolbox.org/form_meta.html) and the [ODK form audit log](https://docs.getodk.org/form-audit-log/), have the detail.

## Photos protect people

Take up to three photos per observation, of the object, not of people. Three is a maximum, not a target.

- Avoid identifiable faces, children and vulnerable people.
- Do not photograph inside safe spaces, health consultations or registration desks.
- A photo is evidence for the map, not a portrait.

Keep voice notes short, and prefer a typed note.

## Send every day

Finalise each form at the object, then send whenever there is a connection, and at least every evening. Each evening the data focal point compares the day's count per collector with what they say they collected (see [Daily review and changes](/guide/review)).

How collection works in the app: [Collecting data using KoboCollect](https://support.kobotoolbox.org/data_collection_kobocollect.html).

:::failure Common failure
Collectors keep forms as drafts "to finish later" and never finalise them. Finalise at the object.
:::

:::tip Other ways this step fails
Points cluster around the vehicle or the camp entrance because they were taken from a distance. Statuses come from what people say rather than what collectors see. Faces of children or survivors end up on a printed map. Brief the rules again when the daily review shows any of these.
:::
