Handover

From surge support to national ownership, in three stages.

Surge teams arrive, assess and leave. RISA is designed so the capacity stays: each deployment trains the people who will run the next one.

  1. Stage 1

    Consortium-led

    National counterparts shadow every step of a deployment.

  2. Stage 2

    Co-led

    National teams lead, with remote support from the consortium.

  3. Stage 3

    Nationally led

    The consortium is the backstop for large or complex events.

A Training of Trainers course prepares a national cohort, who then train enumerators, site managers and drone teams. Every course starts with a SIMEX, a full simulation on realistic scenario data. Drone capacity is built with the Flying Labs network of local operators. TBC

Why RISA

The first days decide the response. They’re also when we know least.

1

The first 72 hours

Access routes, site layout and damage are often unknown, out of date or anecdotal, just when the most important decisions are made.

2

Integration, not technology

Drones, satellites and mobile data collection already exist. They are deployed separately, so the picture arrives late or in pieces.

3

Capacity that stays

RISA is built to be handed over, so national teams can run the next assessment themselves, with the toolbox and training to do it.

The method

Three inputs, one common operational picture.

RISA records what physically exists on the ground. It complements, rather than replaces, MIRA and rapid needs assessments.

  • Aerial imagery

    A drone orthomosaic of the site, flown at 120 m for about 4 cm per pixel, or recent satellite imagery where flying isn’t possible.

    HI/Atlas Logistique · UAV operations

  • Field observation

    A standard KoboCollect survey, recorded by direct observation rather than interviews, that works offline and syncs when there’s signal.

    Kobo · field data

  • GIS integration

    Both brought together on one map with common symbology, a georeferenced inventory of every water point, latrine and service.

    MapAction · GIS

Six steps, from mission preparation to updates

  1. 01

    Mission preparation

    Scope, permits, flight authorisation and the GEO zone unlock, done before travelling.

  2. 02

    Field data collection

    Drone flights and observation surveys, in parallel.

  3. 03

    Data processing

    Orthomosaic stitching and survey cleaning.

  4. 04

    GIS integration

    Layers, symbology and the map layout.

  5. 05

    Dissemination

    To UNDAC, the national authority, clusters and partners.

  6. 06

    Updating

    Follow-up a week later, then as the site changes.
