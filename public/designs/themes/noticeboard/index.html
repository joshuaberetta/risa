RISA: Rapid Infrastructure and Service Assessment

A rapid assessment method that turns drone imagery and direct field observation into one operational map of a displacement site, within days of the site being set up.

About the method

RISA answers three questions in the first days after a sudden-onset emergency:

  1. Can we reach people? Access routes, by status.
  2. Which services still work? Water, sanitation, health, protection.
  3. Where do we act first? Priority gaps, ranked.

It records what physically exists on the ground. It complements, rather than replaces, MIRA and rapid needs assessments.

Why it is needed

  • The first 72 hours. Access routes, site layout and damage are often unknown, out of date or anecdotal, just when the most important decisions are made.
  • Integration, not technology. Drones, satellites and mobile data collection already exist. They are deployed separately, so the picture arrives late or in pieces.
  • Capacity that stays. Surge teams arrive, assess and leave. RISA is built to be handed over, so national teams can run the next assessment themselves.

Three inputs

InputWhat it isLead
Aerial imageryA drone orthomosaic of the site, flown at 120 m for about 4 cm per pixel, or recent satellite imagery where flying isn’t possible.HI/Atlas Logistique
Field observationA standard KoboCollect survey, recorded by direct observation rather than interviews. Works offline.Kobo
GIS integrationBoth brought together on one map with common symbology, and a georeferenced inventory of every service.MapAction

Six steps

  1. Mission preparation. Scope, permits, flight authorisation and the GEO zone unlock, done before travelling.
  2. Field data collection. Drone flights and observation surveys, in parallel.
  3. Data processing. Orthomosaic stitching and survey cleaning.
  4. GIS integration. Layers, symbology and the map layout.
  5. Dissemination. To UNDAC, the national authority, clusters and partners.
  6. Updating. Follow-up a week later, then as the site changes.

Timing: initial assessment 3–5 days after the site is set up, a follow-up about a week later, then updates as the site evolves.

The product

A three-page, mission-ready document that a coordinator can brief from in under a minute. It works printed and offline. An interactive version is being piloted alongside it.

  • Page 1: The map

    Site layout, access routes by status, services, hazards and logistics points.

  • Page 2: Key findings

    Access, essential services and priority gaps, with who should act.

  • Page 3: Hotspot (optional)

    A close-up with before and after imagery and recommended actions.

Training and handover

A Training of Trainers course prepares a national cohort, who then train enumerators, site managers and drone teams. Every course starts with a SIMEX: a full simulation on realistic scenario data.

  1. Consortium-led. National counterparts shadow every step of a deployment.
  2. Co-led. National teams lead, with remote support from the consortium.
  3. Nationally led. The consortium is the backstop for large or complex events.

Drone capacity is built with the Flying Labs network of local operators. TBC
