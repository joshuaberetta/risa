Rapid Infrastructure and Service Assessment

RISA is a method for mapping a displacement site in the first days after it is set up. A small team combines drone imagery with direct field observation and produces a three-page map product that answers three questions.

  1. Can we reach people? Access routes, by status.
  2. Which services still work? Water, sanitation, health and protection.
  3. Where do we act first? Priority gaps, ranked.

Why RISA

The first days after a disaster decide much of the response, and they are when responders know least. Access routes, site layouts and damage are often unknown, out of date or anecdotal.

The tools to fill that gap already exist. Drones, satellite imagery and mobile data collection are deployed separately, by different actors, so the picture arrives late or in pieces. And surge teams arrive, assess and leave, so the capacity leaves with them.

RISA brings these inputs into one standard product, and is built to be handed over so national teams can run the next assessment themselves.

The method

RISA records what physically exists on the ground. It complements, rather than replaces, MIRA and rapid needs assessments. Each assessment combines three inputs.

InputWhat it isLead
Aerial imageryA drone orthomosaic of the site, flown at 120 m for about 4 cm per pixel, or recent satellite imagery where flying isn’t possible.HI/Atlas Logistique
Field observationA standard KoboCollect survey, recorded by direct observation rather than interviews. Works offline and syncs when there’s a connection.Kobo
GIS integrationBoth brought together on one map with common symbology, and a georeferenced inventory of every water point, latrine and service.MapAction

Six steps

  1. Mission preparation. Scope, permits, flight authorisation and the GEO zone unlock, done before travelling.
  2. Field data collection. Drone flights and observation surveys, in parallel.
  3. Data processing. Orthomosaic stitching and survey cleaning.
  4. GIS integration. Layers, symbology and the map layout.
  5. Dissemination. To UNDAC, the national authority, clusters and partners.
  6. Updating. A follow-up a week later, then as the site changes.

The product

Each assessment produces three pages, designed to be read in under a minute. It is a mission-ready document, not a research report. It works printed and offline, and an interactive version is being piloted alongside it.

  1. Page 1, the map. Site layout, access routes by status, services, hazards and logistics points.
  2. Page 2, key findings. Access, essential services and priority gaps, so a coordinator can brief from it.
  3. Page 3, hotspot (optional). A close-up with before and after imagery and recommended actions.

Training and handover

A Training of Trainers course prepares a national cohort, who then train enumerators, site managers and drone teams. Every course starts with a SIMEX: a full simulation on realistic scenario data. Drone capacity is built with the Flying Labs network of local operators. TBC

Handover happens in three stages:

  1. Consortium-led. National counterparts shadow every step of a deployment.
  2. Co-led. National teams lead, with remote support from the consortium.
  3. Nationally led. The consortium is the backstop for large or complex events.

Adopt RISA

The consortium can help a national emergency management authority scope a deployment, run a Training of Trainers course and adapt the toolbox. RISA products carry the authority’s name first.

Developed by HI/Atlas Logistique, MapAction and Kobo. Used in Mozambique (SADC SIMEX) and in the Venezuela earthquake response.

RISA is an open methodology developed by HI/Atlas Logistique, MapAction and Kobo. Nothing is patented. Use it and adapt it.
